## The split: which years train and which years test

The split is **by year**, not a random shuffle: **2014, 2015, 2016, 2017 train** and **2018, 2019, 2020, 2021 test**. The boundary is the project's fixed policy (the first half of the years available train, the later half test), applied by `config.split_years`, so no module chooses it with a positional index. It was fixed before the Step 2 drift results were examined and those results did not change it.

- Training rows: **72,000** (36,000 benign, 36,000 malware).
- Test rows: **27,000** (13,500 benign, 13,500 malware).
- Together that is 99,000 rows, the whole dataset loaded in Step 1, so no row is dropped or counted twice.

The test years are all later than every training year, which is what makes this a time-based split: the models are asked to do what a deployed detector has to do, namely judge apps from years it has never seen. Step 6 reports the test years one at a time, so the effect of the drift measured in Step 2 is visible year by year.

## Scaling

The features are non-negative integer API-call counts over 1848 columns, and their ranges differ by orders of magnitude: in the training years the largest single count is 49,110, while the per-feature mean count runs from 0 to 2.73e+03. A model that treats all coefficients alike would therefore be dominated by a handful of common calls. Two preprocessing steps are applied, in this order:

1. **Drop never-varying features** (`VarianceThreshold(threshold=0.0)`): a column that holds one value across the training years carries no information for a model and would give a zero standard deviation to divide by.
2. **Standardise** (`StandardScaler`): subtract the mean and divide by the standard deviation, so every feature is on a comparable scale.

Of the 1848 features, **743 never vary in the training years and are dropped, leaving 1105**. The same 1105 columns, in the same order, are used for the test years.

91 of those 743 dropped features are constant in the training years but vary in the test years. That is expected and is not a mistake: the decision is made on training evidence only, because a deployed model cannot consult future data. What this shows is a change in feature *variability* across the split, which is consistent with the temporal changes Step 2 examined; on its own it does not establish the magnitude or the statistical significance of any feature's drift (the KS statistics and adjusted p-values in Step 2 do that). It is a reason to compare a static model with retraining in Step 7.

Scaling is not forced on every model, because not every model benefits from it:

| model                  | StandardScaler   | reason                                                                                              |
|:-----------------------|:-----------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| LogisticRegression     | yes              | regularised linear model: the penalty treats all coefficients alike, so features must share a scale |
| LinearSVC              | yes              | margin depends on distances, so unscaled count ranges dominate                                      |
| SGDClassifier          | yes              | gradient steps need comparable feature scales to converge                                           |
| RandomForestClassifier | no               | splits are on thresholds per feature; monotone rescaling does not change the tree                   |

Random Forest is fitted on the same dropped-column set but without the scaler, so the comparison between models differs only where the model actually requires it.

## How leakage between years is prevented

1. **Both learned steps live inside one `Pipeline`.** Dropping constant columns and scaling are estimators, not a one-off transformation of the whole table, so refitting the pipeline refits both on whatever rows it is given and nothing else.
2. **The pipeline is fitted on the training years only** (2014, 2015, 2016, 2017) and applied unchanged to 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021. The test years are transformed, never fitted on, so no test-year mean, standard deviation or column decision reaches the model.
3. **Model selection uses time-ordered (forward-chaining) validation inside the training years**, never random k-fold, which would put later years in a fold that trains on them. The folds are:
  1. train on 2014 -> validate on 2015
  2. train on 2014, 2015 -> validate on 2016
  3. train on 2014, 2015, 2016 -> validate on 2017
   Each fold refits the pipeline on its own training years, so in the last fold nothing is fitted on 2017, and no fold touches a test year.
4. **No row ever crosses the boundary.** The split is taken by the `year` column with `config.split_years`, and `check_split` fails if a year appears on both sides, if a test year is not later than every training year, or if the two parts do not add up to the rows loaded.
5. **Metadata cannot reach a model.** `features_labels` refuses to return a feature matrix that still holds `apkname`, `year`, `label` or `source_file`, so the app identifier and the year itself are never features.
6. **Step 2 did not influence any of this.** The drift statistics are descriptive; they were not used to choose the boundary, select features or set any parameter.

## Checks run on this step

These are the builder's own checks, printed by the step and repeated here; the tester agent's independent tests are in `tests/`. Each one is listed with what it does and does not establish, so no single number is read as proof that the pipeline is leakage-free.

- **The fitted scaler stores the training set's own statistics.** Recomputing the mean and the population standard deviation of the retained columns from the training rows and comparing them with the fitted `StandardScaler` gives a largest absolute difference of 0.000e+00 (mean) and 0.000e+00 (scale). *Establishes:* the stored statistics are those of the training rows. *Does not establish:* that no test row influenced fitting - that follows from fitting on the training frame only, which is point 2 of the previous section, not from this comparison.
- **Transforming the training rows gives what standardising should give.** Across the 72,000 training rows, the average of the retained features' transformed means is +0.000 and the average of their transformed standard deviations is 1.000. These are averages over per-feature statistics, not the mean and standard deviation of the whole matrix taken as one pool. *Establishes:* the transform does centre and scale the data it was fitted on. *Does not establish:* anything about the test years, which are deliberately not standardised to their own statistics.
- **Shapes and column identity line up.** The training matrix is 72,000 x 1,848 before preprocessing and 72,000 x 1,105 after; the test matrix is 27,000 x 1,105 after. `check_feature_contract` confirms train and test offer the same feature names in the same order, and `check_feature_lists` confirms the kept and dropped lists are disjoint and together account for every feature.
- **The year lists partition the dataset.** `check_split` confirms each year is on exactly one side, that none is omitted or invented, that every test year is later than every training year, and that the two frames add up to the rows loaded in Step 1.
- **Labels and years belong to the rows they describe.** `check_labels_years` confirms the label and year series returned with each matrix match that split's frame and hold only the two known label values, and `split_summary` refuses to summarise an unexpected label rather than quietly reporting counts that do not add up.

Two pipelines are saved rather than one, because the scaling policy above has exactly two distinct configurations: the saved artifacts therefore represent the policy instead of implying that every model is scaled.

## What Step 4 uses from here

`split_by_year` for the frames, `features_labels` for a metadata-free feature matrix, `preprocessor_for(model_name)` for the pipeline that model needs, and `forward_chaining_folds` / `fold_indices` for the `cv` passed to model selection.

**The saved pipelines above are diagnostic artifacts, not inputs to model selection.** Step 4 must not load one and transform the data before selecting a model: that would fit the dropped-column set and the scaler once on all of 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017 and then reuse those statistics inside every validation fold, so each fold's validation year would have helped set them. Instead Step 4 builds a fresh pipeline per model with `preprocessor_for(model_name)`, puts the classifier in the same `Pipeline`, and passes the folds as `cv`, so scikit-learn refits the preprocessing on each fold's training years and only transforms that fold's validation year. The test years take no part in model selection at all; the final pipeline is fitted on the full training half once, after the model is chosen.
